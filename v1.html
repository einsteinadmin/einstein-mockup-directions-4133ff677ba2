Three scroll moments that carry the brand

  • Local SEO. A story-led homepage can dilute keyword weight unless branch pages pick up the slack. KP needs to scope city landing pages aggressively in parallel.
  • Aging. Origin-story-first can feel dated after 2 years if it never evolves. Plan for an annual "next chapter" refresh built into the theme.

Three scroll moments that carry the brand

  • The mosaic. Twelve real reviews in the hero — auto-refreshing weekly. Visitors realize within 3 seconds they're looking at the whole iceberg, not the tip.
  • "Of every 100 movers who apply, 8 make the cut." The hiring funnel as a number is the bridge between "look at the reviews" and "now you know why."

Risks and trade-offs

  • Cold start. No story, no warmth on landing — it's facts-first. A skeptic loves it. A nervous first-time mover may find it transactional. Pair with strong photography in branch pages.
  • Tech lift. Live filterable review wall needs an ingestion pipeline (Google + Yelp + Facebook APIs → WP custom post type or static-cached endpoint). KP needs a dev partner or Matisen's team owns the integration.
  • Number maintenance. "8,100+ reviews · 4.9 stars" needs to be live or stale numbers will torch the credibility. Build a nightly fetch into the FSE theme.
  • Doesn't differentiate on personality alone. Honey Homes/Lemonade/Oscar all do proof-first too. We win on receipts being deeper than theirs, not on the form itself.

  • Existing per-branch Google review counts
  • Quote form — geo-defaults the "where" field

Change

  • Homepage from one-size-fits-all → ten near-homepages, one block theme
  • BM is a name in a footer → BM is the hero on their market's homepage
  • "Locations" link in nav → defaults to user's city + dropdown to switch
  • National stats demoted; local stats promoted

Kill

  • The single national homepage (it becomes a /about-style fallback for visitors w/o geo)
  • Generic stock hero photos
  • "6 Reasons" — replaced by "Why Austinites pick Einstein" / "Why Tampa picks Einstein" / etc.

Three scroll moments that carry the brand

  • "You're in Austin · change city ▾" The pill at the top of the hero is the whole concept in one widget. Tells the visitor instantly: this site knows where you are, and we run THIS place.
  • "I've moved 12,000 people in this town. Yours is next." Calvin (or whichever BM) saying it. Makes the company human at the scale where humans actually live.
  • The neighborhood tiles. Mueller / East / South Congress — proves we know the city, not just have an address in it. Same play in every market: Tampa gets Hyde Park / Seminole Heights / South Tampa, DFW gets Plano / Uptown / Bishop Arts.

Risks and trade-offs

  • Content production multiplies by 10. Every block theme update needs ten copy passes, ten photo shoots, ten neighborhood guides. Without Austin L scaled up or a content ops hire, this rots.
  • BM turnover. If Calvin is the hero of the Austin homepage and Calvin leaves, the page has to swap fast. Need a "BM-spotlight" block pattern that's truly modular.
  • Geo-detection edge cases. Out-of-market visitors (a Dallas customer researching Tampa for a parent's move) need a clean "show me a different city" flow. Don't lock anyone in.
  • SEO complexity. Ten near-homepages risks duplicate-content penalties unless schemas, canonicals, and local entities are scoped tight. KP needs to scope this carefully.

Inspiration leaning

This is the Lemonade playbook — productized service, system on display, transparent at every step. Plus Honey Homes' narrative arc (Problem → Solution → Benefit → Proof → Guarantee) and Sweetgreen's editorial restraint. Voice is still Squatch — confident, irreverent, not corporate.

Preserve

  • "The Smart Move" external positioning + "Smart Move System" naming
  • "Tiny math elves" / "No PhD required" lines, placed under the Quote step
  • The new quote form — but it's now Step 1, not a sidebar
  • Variable Pricing infrastructure (the system is the story)

Change

  • Site IA from "Services / About / Locations" → "The System / Locations / Reviews"
  • "6 Reasons" → 5 system steps, each clickable to a deeper page
  • Hero from emotional → demonstrational (the diagram IS the hero)
  • Frame ourselves as a product company that does service, not a service company with a website

Kill

  • Generic "Why Choose Us" framing
  • The disconnected awards carousel (numbers attach to specific steps)
  • Brand-story block on the homepage (lives at /about — see Concept 01 if you want it home)

Three scroll moments that carry the brand

  • The hero diagram. Five nodes labeled Quote / Crew / Move / Price / After. Each lights up as you scroll past it. The whole page is a system tour.
  • "Quote $1,840. Invoice $1,840." The Step 4 visual. A side-by-side that names the entire industry's worst behavior and shows we don't do it.
  • "If something breaks, here's exactly what happens." The Step 5 reframe. We talk about claims before the customer worries about them. Trust at its bravest.

Risks and trade-offs

  • Loses warmth. System-as-hero is intellectually compelling but emotionally cooler than Concept 01. Movers don't always shop with their head. Pair with strong photography to keep humans in the frame.
  • The system has to actually work. Quote-to-invoice match, 99.4% on-time, $1.66/hr damages — every number on the homepage becomes a public commitment. Operations gets graded by the website.
  • Trademark exposure. "Smart Move System" goes from internal phrase to homepage hero. Worth confirming TM filing status before launch.
  • FSE complexity. A "step block with active-state animation tied to scroll" is the most custom-block-heavy of the five concepts. Higher KP build cost than the others.

  • 8,100+ / 4.9★ / 55+ awards — repositioned as masthead-style numbers

Change

  • Photography from stock to commissioned editorial (black + white moments work hard)
  • Typography to custom or carefully paired (a serif headline + clean sans is the whole game)

Kill

  • Awards carousel
  • "6 Reasons" linear list
  • Repeating bottom-of-page CTA bar
  • The blog teaser on home (becomes the "In This Issue" block)

Three scroll moments that carry the brand

  • "Moving is awful. We've spent 22 years making sure ours isn't." The cover line. Names the customer's actual feeling before pitching anything. Earns the rest of the scroll.
  • The piano photo. One full-bleed editorial image where a crew is wrapping something precious. Anchors the whole hospitality thesis in three seconds.
  • The cities contents page. Setting up the branch directory like a magazine TOC reframes 10 locations as a publication's footprint, not a service-area map.

Risks and trade-offs

  • Conversion-rate skeptic's worst nightmare. Whitespace and editorial framing test poorly against high-intent paid-search traffic that wants a form, fast. Mitigation: paid-search lands on /get-quote/ directly, this concept is the brand-organic landing.
  • Photography budget is real. The whole concept fails on stock imagery. Need a commissioned shoot in at least 3 cities (Austin, Tampa, Dallas) before launch. Austin L can produce, but not solo.
  • Typography licensing. A custom or paid serif/sans pairing is part of the differentiation. Adobe Fonts license can carry it, but lock the choice early — changing fonts post-build is painful.
  • Reads "premium." Could nudge perception toward higher-priced. That may help our positioning ("white-glove from work boots") and may hurt the bargain shopper. Test against branch-level conversion before scaling everywhere.
